01 / Workshop Overview

Motivation

The rapid development of embodied artificial intelligence is transforming how autonomous robots perceive, reason, learn, and interact with complex physical environments.

Foundation models, vision-language-action (VLA) models, world models, multimodal reasoning, and agentic AI are increasingly enabling robots to move beyond task-specific autonomy toward systems capable of understanding their surroundings, reasoning over long horizons, adapting to uncertainty, and coordinating actions through persistent experience.

Aerospace robotics provides a particularly demanding and scientifically important setting for advancing these capabilities. Planetary rovers, aerial vehicles, spacecraft, robotic manipulators, and heterogeneous multi-robot teams must operate under partial observability, communication delays, limited energy and computing resources, uncertain terrain and contact dynamics, and environments where human intervention may be delayed or unavailable.

Trustworthy Spatial-Temporal Autonomy means that an embodied intelligent system should not reason only from its instantaneous observation. Instead, it should construct, maintain, and update representations of where it has been, what it has observed, what actions it has taken, how the environment has changed, what remains uncertain, and what it should investigate next.

The workshop will investigate how emerging embodied-intelligence techniques can enable the transition from reactive autonomy toward persistent, adaptive, trustworthy, and science-aware aerospace robotic intelligence.

A dedicated Quantum-Augmented Embodied Robotics track will explore whether quantum, quantum-inspired, and hybrid quantum-classical methods can complement classical embodied intelligence for high-dimensional representation, uncertainty modeling, planning, optimization, and multi-agent coordination.

02 / Scope and Technical Themes

Research Topics

Embodied Foundation Models and VLA Systems

Foundation models for robotics, VLA architectures, multimodal representation learning, language-conditioned control, embodied reasoning, adaptation, and generalization to unseen aerospace environments.

Spatial-Temporal World Models and Persistent Memory

Metric-semantic maps, episodic memory, predictive world models, latent dynamics, memory-augmented architectures, continual environmental updating, uncertainty-aware memory, and trustworthy world-model learning.

Long-Horizon Reasoning and Planning

Hierarchical planning, multimodal reasoning, agentic autonomy, predictive planning, embodied chain-of-action reasoning, and integration between learned representations and conventional planning systems.

Trustworthy and Uncertainty-Aware Autonomy

Uncertainty quantification, runtime assurance, verification and validation (V&V), safety constraints, formal verification, risk-aware planning, anomaly detection, failure recovery, and verified fallback behaviors.

Navigation, Mobility, and Manipulation

Autonomous rover navigation, aerial mobility, spacecraft operations, contact-rich manipulation, sampling, instrument placement, construction, inspection, maintenance, and assembly.

Multi-Agent and Distributed Embodied Intelligence

Shared spatial-temporal memory, decentralized decision-making, communication-aware coordination, collaborative perception, distributed sensing, task allocation, adaptive team reconfiguration, and uncertainty-aware coordination.

Science-Driven Autonomous Exploration

Science-aware planning, active perception, information-gain objectives, autonomous hypothesis refinement, adaptive sensing, distributed scientific measurements, and closed-loop exploration.

Observation → Representation → Hypothesis → Uncertainty → Action → New Measurement

Efficient and Resource-Aware Embodied AI

Model compression, efficient inference, edge AI, adaptive computation, hardware-aware learning, neuromorphic approaches, distributed computation, and hybrid onboard/ground architectures.

03 / Special Track

Quantum-Augmented Embodied Robotics

This dedicated track will explore the intersection of quantum computing, quantum-inspired algorithms, embodied intelligence, and aerospace robotics.

Rather than positioning quantum computation as a replacement for classical embodied AI, the track will investigate where quantum, quantum-inspired, and hybrid quantum-classical methods may augment selected components of embodied robotic intelligence.

  • Quantum and quantum-inspired representation learning
  • Quantum-enhanced world models and latent-state representations
  • Hybrid quantum-classical learning architectures
  • Quantum optimization for trajectory planning and task allocation
  • Quantum uncertainty representation and probabilistic reasoning
  • Quantum machine learning for multimodal perception
  • Quantum reinforcement learning and decision-making
  • Quantum-inspired robotic optimization
  • Quantum-enhanced multi-agent intelligence
  • Aerospace mission planning and resource allocation
  • Classical versus quantum benchmarking
  • Hardware, simulation, and scalability
Where can quantum computation meaningfully augment embodied robotic intelligence, and how should such advantages be evaluated against strong classical approaches?

The track will explicitly distinguish among classical, quantum-inspired, simulated quantum, and physical quantum implementations to support rigorous and reproducible evaluation.

04 / Central Research Questions

Questions We Aim to Address

1

Representation: What spatial-temporal representations allow embodied agents to preserve and reason over experience across long-duration aerospace missions?

2

Reasoning: How can foundation models and world models support long-horizon reasoning rather than only immediate perception-to-action mappings?

3

Trustworthiness: How can learned world models, VLA systems, and agentic components be verified, validated, monitored, and constrained for safety- and mission-critical operations?

4

Adaptation: How should autonomous systems use uncertainty and new observations to modify goals, plans, sensing strategies, and robot configurations?

5

Coordination: How can multiple embodied agents build and act upon shared spatial-temporal representations while operating under communication and resource constraints?

6

Autonomous Science: How can scientific hypotheses, uncertainty, and information gain directly influence autonomous sensing and exploration decisions?

7

Efficiency: How can advanced embodied-intelligence models operate within computation, communication, energy, and thermal constraints?

8

Quantum Augmentation: Which components could meaningfully benefit from quantum or quantum-inspired computation, and how should these benefits be evaluated?

9

Deployment: What benchmarks, simulation environments, hardware platforms, terrestrial analogs, and flight-relevant experiments are needed to move embodied AI toward aerospace missions?

05 / Relevance to ICRA

Why This Workshop at ICRA

ICRA provides a natural venue because these questions span rapidly growing communities including robot learning, embodied AI, foundation models, field robotics, space robotics, autonomous navigation, manipulation, multi-robot systems, planning, trustworthy AI, and emerging computational paradigms.

The workshop will strengthen connections between the embodied-intelligence community, which is developing increasingly general learning architectures, and the aerospace robotics community, which provides demanding real-world problems involving long-duration autonomy, limited resources, uncertainty, safety, and delayed communication.

The workshop seeks to establish Trustworthy Spatial-Temporal Autonomy as a cross-cutting research direction connecting these communities.

10 / Vision

From Reactive Autonomy to Persistent Embodied Intelligence

The long-term vision of this workshop is to move aerospace autonomy from systems that primarily react to the present toward embodied intelligent systems that remember the past, understand the present, anticipate the future, quantify what they do not know, and autonomously decide what to observe and do next.

Trustworthy Spatial-Temporal Autonomy provides a framework for this transition.

By integrating persistent representations, multimodal perception, world modeling, embodied reasoning, uncertainty awareness, trustworthy execution, and multi-agent coordination, future aerospace robots could become adaptive and trustworthy partners in exploration rather than remote platforms executing predetermined sequences.

The Quantum-Augmented Embodied Robotics track will additionally explore whether emerging computational paradigms can augment selected aspects of this intelligence, from representation and uncertainty to optimization and coordination, while demanding rigorous evidence against strong classical methods.
